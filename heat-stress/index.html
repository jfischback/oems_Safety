Heat Stress Screening

Pick the standard, enter the readings, then press Calculate.

OSHA has no final heat standard. This uses the triggers in OSHA's proposed rule, 29 CFR 1910.148 (published August 30, 2024), which is not in effect.

Work

Rest (the rest of the hour)

Calculate WBGT from thermometer readings

Indoors: WBGT = 0.7 NWB + 0.3 GT. Outdoors in sun: WBGT = 0.7 NWB + 0.2 GT + 0.1 DB (NIOSH 2016-106, Section 9.3.2).

How it works

NIOSH. RAL (unacclimatized) = 59.9 − 14.1 log10M and REL (acclimatized) = 56.7 − 11.5 log10M, in °C-WBGT, with M in watts (NIOSH 2016-106, Section 8.1). WBGT and M are 1-hour time-weighted averages that include rest periods (Sections 1.1.2 and 1.1.3). 1 kcal/h = 1.16 W.

OSHA (proposed). Initial heat trigger: heat index of 80 °F, or a WBGT equal to the NIOSH RAL. High heat trigger: heat index of 90 °F, or a WBGT equal to the NIOSH REL. Heat index is the National Weather Service heat index. Exposures of 15 minutes or less in any 60-minute period are outside the proposed rule.

Limits of this screen. The NIOSH limits are for a 70 kg (154 lb) worker in one layer of normal work clothing. They do not cover heavier protective clothing; NIOSH notes that when vapor-impermeable clothing is worn, dry bulb temperature is a better measure than WBGT.

For screening only. Not a substitute for an evaluation by a qualified industrial hygienist or a heat illness prevention program. Measure WBGT at the worker's position. State heat rules may apply and may be stricter.
